# Rockabilly Roasting Co. — Brand Voice Guide

## Who We Are

Rockabilly Roasting is a craft coffee roaster rooted in Kennewick, WA. We take our coffee seriously and our community personally. The rockabilly aesthetic isn't a costume — it's a sensibility: handmade over mass-produced, character over polish, real over flashy.

We're not trying to be the loudest brand in the room. We're trying to be the most reliable one.

---

## Voice in Three Words

**Warm. Knowledgeable. Unpretentious.**

We know our craft inside and out, and we share that knowledge like a friend who happens to be an expert — not like a lecturer, and not like a hype man.

---

## Core Voice Principles

### 1. Craft comes first, cool comes second

We lead with what's in the cup. Origin, roast profile, flavor notes, process — these are interesting because *coffee is interesting*, not because we dressed it up in rockabilly imagery.

**Do:** "Chop Top is a dark roast built for people who want weight and warmth without bitterness. Indonesian beans, slow roasted."

**Don't:** "Rebel against boring coffee with our dangerously smooth dark roast."

---

### 2. Warm, not gushing

We're genuinely glad you're here. But we don't fawn over customers or pile on exclamation points. The warmth is in the specificity — in knowing what you might like, in remembering your last order, in talking to you like a regular even on your first visit.

**Do:** "If you're new to white coffee, start here. It's higher caffeine, lighter roast, and a little nutty. Most people are surprised how much they like it."

**Don't:** "We're SO excited to share this AMAZING coffee with you!!!"

---

### 3. Short sentences earn trust

Long, flowery copy signals insecurity. If the coffee is good, we don't need to oversell it. Say the thing, then stop.

**Do:** "Berry-forward. Bright finish. Built for espresso."

**Don't:** "Embark on a sensory journey as vibrant berry notes and zesty citrus intertwine with a velvety smooth finish that lingers like a sweet dream."

---

### 4. The rockabilly voice is texture, not identity

Product names, visual aesthetic, and occasional cultural nods (*Chop Top, Bike Blend*) carry the brand's personality. The copy doesn't need to work overtime reinforcing it. Let the name do the work; let the description do the selling.

**Do:** "Bike Blend — a smooth, reliable everyday roast. Medium body, low acidity, consistent every morning."

**Don't:** "Rev up your mornings with this rev-your-engine blend that'll have you cruisin' all day, daddy-o."

---

### 5. Community is earned, not performed

We have real customers with names and stories. The warmth in our reviews ("Travis, Laura, and the whole Rockabilly crew") reflects something real. We don't fake that — we protect it. This means no generic "we love our community!" filler copy. We show it through specificity: local delivery days, knowing when someone is a regular, real names.

**Do:** "We deliver Mondays and Thursdays. Order by Sunday night and we'll have it on your doorstep."

**Don't:** "We're more than a coffee company — we're a family, and YOU are part of it!"

---

## Tone by Context

| Context | Tone |
|---|---|
| Product descriptions | Precise, confident, sensory without being florid |
| Email subject lines | Direct, sometimes dry — never clickbait |
| Email body | Conversational, like a note from someone you know |
| Social captions | Brief, grounded, occasionally wry |
| Subscription confirmations | Warm and specific — mention the product by name |
| Error / problem states | Honest and quick to fix — no corporate runaround |

---

## Product Description Template

```
[Product Name]

[One-line roast summary: body, roast level, key characteristic.]

[2–3 sentences: origin or blend notes, flavor profile, best brewing method or use case.]

[Optional: one sentence that earns a personal recommendation — "good for X type of drinker."]
```

**Example — Cloud 9 Espresso:**

> Cloud 9 is our flagship espresso blend — full-bodied, complex, built for the machine.
>
> Three origins, each roasted to bring out berry brightness and a clean citrus edge. Pulls well as a straight shot or holds up under milk without going flat.
>
> If you're used to one-dimensional espresso, this one will reset your expectations.

## Email / Social Tone Guide

### Subject lines

Keep them factual or gently intriguing. Avoid hype.

| Good | Avoid |
|---|---|
| "Your Chop Top is on the way" | "You're going to LOVE what's coming" |
| "New roast: Bike Blend is back" | "We've been working on something BIG..." |
| "Monday delivery reminder" | "Don't miss out — last chance!!!" |
| "A note from the roaster" | "The Rockabilly family has news" |

### Social captions

Lead with the coffee. Keep it short. One idea per post.

**Do:**
> White coffee — roasted lighter, runs higher caffeine, tastes like something between coffee and toasted grain. Not for everyone. Definitely for some of you.

**Don't:**
> We LOVE our white coffee and we know you will too! Life's too short for boring coffee, am I right?! #coffeelover #specialty #rockabilly

### Subscription emails

Always name the product. Acknowledge the repeat purchase without being sycophantic.

**Do:**
> Your Bike Blend subscription ships Thursday. Same great bag — we haven't changed a thing, which is kind of the point.

**Don't:**
> Thank you SO much for being a loyal subscriber! Your support means the world to us and we can't wait for you to enjoy your coffee!

---

## What We Never Do

- Use emojis. Ever. In any context.
- Overuse exclamation points
- Use "journey," "experience," "elevate," or "artisanal" without irony
- Write copy that could belong to any other coffee brand
- Fake urgency ("order NOW before it's gone!")
- Pad product descriptions to sound more premium
- Perform community rather than demonstrate it
